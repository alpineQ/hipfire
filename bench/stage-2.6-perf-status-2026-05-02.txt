## Measured perf

  Variant     N    Wall      Per-dispatch  Per-iter
  score_one   1    0.45 ms   0.45 ms       450 us
  score_layer 128  26.84 ms  26.84 ms      210 us

The 2x amortization from N=1 to N=128 is much smaller than the
dequant kernel's amortization (which dropped from 67 us to ~3 us
per iter). The score kernel's per-iter compute is ~50x larger
than dequant's, so the host-overhead floor amortizes less.

## Projection to full layer at production scale (N=32768)

Single-core:
  per-dispatch  = 32768 * 210 us / 128 = 53.7 s (literally seconds)
  per-token     = 46 * 53.7 s = 2470 seconds (catastrophic)

Even at hypothetical 16-core SIMD-optimized:
  per-iter assumed ~6.5 us (32x speedup vs scalar)
  per-dispatch  = 32768 * 6.5 us / 16 = 13.3 ms
  per-token     = 46 * 13.3 ms = 612 ms (9x slower than iGPU 67 ms)

The 14% lift projection in docs/plans/asym3-fused-score-plan.md
assumed ~100 cycles per transcendental and a much smaller per-iter
budget than what we measured. Reality:
  - per-iter cost is ~3000 cycles (vs estimated ~322)
  - mostly dominated by Givens scalar loop + sqrt + accumulation
  - SIMD vectorization across the 128 bands per (head, pos) is
    the only path to closing the gap

## Remaining work for stage 2.6 lift

  1. SIMD vectorize the Givens + sqrt + accumulation loop. Target
     16x speedup via aie::vector<float, 16> ops. ~4-8 hours focused.
  2. Multi-core fan-out (mirror dequant 8c topology). ~2-4 hours.
  3. Per-layer broadcast: the c_mag / c_abs / cos_a / sin_a /
     cos_t / sin_t arrays are constant across iters within a
     dispatch. Currently re-shipped 128 times per dispatch (3072
     bytes per iter unnecessary). Splitting into a constant
     ObjectFifo + per-iter ObjectFifo reduces input BW 26x. The
     remaining 2 input DMA channels accommodate this. ~2-3 hours.
  4. Multi-shape variants OR runtime-N parameterization, plus
     engine wiring + prefetch. ~6-12 hours.

Total: 14-27 hours of focused authoring + bench work to get a
concrete stage 1.5 / 2.6 lift number (which may or may not beat
iGPU baseline; the math suggests it's close).

## Honest assessment

The session has shipped substantial correctness infrastructure for
both stage 1.4 dequant and stage 2.6 score. The kernels work.
What's NOT shipped: a kernel fast enough to beat iGPU at full
production shape. The math doesn't promise an easy win even with
all four optimizations stacked.

Recommended next moves (in priority order):
  1. SIMD vectorize the score kernel + measure. If per-iter drops
     to <30 us, stage 2.6 becomes plausibly competitive.
  2. If SIMD alone doesn't close the gap, add multi-core + per-
     layer broadcast optimizations and re-measure.
  3. If still not competitive, escalate to MANUAL_REVIEW with
     full telemetry. Stage 2.6 may simply not be feasible on the
     AIE-2P NPU2 for this workload, in which case the npu-roadmap
     stops here without a default flip.
